# Spiral energy-management training

The seventh flight-lab scenario introduces a deterministic developed right
turn after eight seconds. It sets an initial brake/weight-shift state and
angular/vertical energy, then returns authority entirely to the normal
keyboard or controller inputs.

The objective is to release the inside brake progressively, neutralize weight
shift, reduce bank and stabilize pitch after the exit. Coaching distinguishes:

- excessive load or canopy deformation: ease the inside brake;
- sustained yaw/roll: progressively reduce bank;
- the first low-load, low-rate phase after rotation stops: stabilize the exit;
- stable flight: active piloting.

Scoring records peak load, time above 3.5 g, time to a stable exit, yaw/roll
rates, pitch roughness, recovery surge, deformation and other canopy incidents.
Remaining in a high-load spiral therefore scores substantially worse than a
controlled progressive exit. A deterministic physics test also performs a
progressive release using ordinary controls and requires low-rate, sub-2 g
flight afterward.

This exercise is simulator training, not instructional authority for real
flight. Entry conditions and scoring thresholds remain research assumptions
pending qualified SIV-instructor and instrumented-flight review.
